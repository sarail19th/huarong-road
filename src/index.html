About this game

華容道 · Huáróng Dào

The story

In 208 AD the warlord Cao Cao lost the Battle of Red Cliffs and fled with what was left of his army. In the classic novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms, the only way home was the narrow Huarong Road — and waiting there to stop him was Guan Yu, sworn brother of his enemy Liu Bei. But Cao Cao had once treated Guan Yu with great kindness. Torn between duty and honour, Guan Yu stepped aside and let him escape.

That scene comes from the novel, not from the history books.

Why a puzzle?

The puzzle turns that escape into a game. The big red block is Cao Cao, every other block is a general or soldier standing in his way, and the gap at the bottom is the road out. In the classic layout:

  • Big red block = Cao Cao
  • Wide block = Guan Yu, who guards the road
  • Tall blocks = the generals Zhang Fei, Zhao Yun, Ma Chao and Huang Zhong
  • Small squares = soldiers

Why “Road”?

華容道 (Huáróng Dào) simply means “Huarong Road”: Huarong is a real place (today Huarong County in Hunan, China) and dào means road or path. In the West the same kind of puzzle is called Klotski (from the Polish klocki, “blocks”), and in France L’Âne Rouge — “the Red Donkey”.

Fun facts

  • The classic starting layout, Heng Dao Li Ma (橫刀立馬, “sabre laid across, horse standing”), takes at least 81 moves if a block may turn corners in one move — and 90 here, where a move is one straight slide.
  • From that start only 25,955 different positions can ever be reached — and the shortest way out is hidden somewhere among them.
  • Every level here is checked by a solver, so par is the true minimum — three stars means you played it perfectly.
  • Nobody knows for sure who first made a sliding-block puzzle. The Three Kingdoms story is the theme it was given, and similar puzzles were known in the West from the early 1900s.
